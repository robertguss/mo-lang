# Fable's reading: generation six, the result

This restates, without change, what was on main at d846a4b. It is short because the page has the tables.

1. **Correctness.** Under the six earlier suites no program has a cause it did not already carry (Python 121, 189, 64, 55, 80, 94; Go 121, 188, 66, 55, 79, 92; Elixir 120, 128 of 129, 63 of 65, 53, 79, 94; Mo 121, 189, 64, 55, 80, 94 in both runtimes). Mo's generation-five defect is fixed (the sixth suite 94 of 94). The sealed seventh suite failed at server start on every program, as it had to: every start used a --retain-ms under the spec's 1,000. Under the corrected defects6b.py: Python 90 of 95, Go 95 of 97, Elixir 95 of 97, Mo 92 of 95, and **Fable attributes every failure to the harness or the machine, none to a program**: two /queues checks that all four programs fail identically (an ambiguous spec sentence), one check that assumes a numeric cutoff in the prune record (Mo and Python write a timestamp, which the spec allows), and Python's bench reading /proc on a Mac.
2. **The weak point of that attribution, said plainly.** The party that wrote the broken suite also wrote the corrected one, after two maintainers had finished and after reading one maintainer's report, and then judged every one of its failures to be its own. Each judgment has a reason an outsider can check (four independent maintainers agreeing; the spec's wording; /proc), but "zero defects in every program under the author's second suite, all failures excused by the author" is the weakest form this result could take, and an independent suite would be worth more than this one.
3. **The predictions.** P1 held; P2 held at a tie of zero; P3 failed (no baseline lost anything under the prune and the kill); P4 failed on its first half (every program opens its own change-5 folder) and its second half, the directory-entry kill, cannot be tested from outside; P5 recorded and compared with nothing (broken sessions, two machines); **P6 failed for the sixth time: no never or invariant tripped on a wrong edit, in Mo or in any baseline by their reports**; P7 is void as a test of discovery because change 6's own status paragraph names the cause, and what remains true is a rate: the lead's alternating rows put Mo's pairs at 32 workers at 1.31 and 1.01 times change 3, Python 1.25 and 1.06, Elixir 1.22 and 0.99, Go inside the noise of a disk-flush-bound path (change 3 itself swings 67 to 104 pairs a second).
4. **Speed across languages on the Mac is not a comparison.** Go's Sync uses F_FULLFSYNC; Mo's runtimes and Python use plain fsync, which on macOS does not reach the disk. The Mac rows compare unlike promises; only each program against its own change 3 means anything there.
5. **The language rule's ledger:** six of ten generations, zero catches, threshold two. Nothing in this generation moves it. The one Mo-only defect of the round was caught by a suite after the fact and fixed from a ticket, not caught by a law.
6. **What the generation is actually evidence of** is the experiment's own instruments: a sealed suite never run before its seal; a runner that swallowed a usage error for a whole generation (generation five's third-suite column was never measured; measured today: Go 65, Python 64, Elixir 63, Mo 64); a simulator without a bound on a perpetual timer (the 13.4 GB process); no Fs row that syncs a folder; fsync on macOS.
7. **Not verified by Fable:** the maintainers' own sequence scripts (their directory-entry kills are read from their reports, not rerun); tokens for any VM segment; whether Elixir's or Python's sync reaches the disk on macOS (only Go's and Mo's sources were read); any suite on a quiet machine (Robert was using the Mac all morning, load 4 to 8).
