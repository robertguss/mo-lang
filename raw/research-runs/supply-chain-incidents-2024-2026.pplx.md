# Software Supply-Chain Incidents in Package Ecosystems, January 2024 – September 12, 2026

**Purpose.** Evidence base for package-manager threat modeling (Mo Lang). Every value below was taken from a page fetched during this research session, and the source link sits next to the value it supports. Cells marked `n.a.` could not be confirmed from a fetched primary source. Where two sources give different numbers for the same thing, both are reported.

**Scope.** npm, PyPI, crates.io, RubyGems, Go modules / proxy.golang.org, Maven Central, NuGet, VS Code Marketplace / Open VSX, GitHub Actions, and GitHub account/repo-level compromise.

**Note on the "Ecosystem mitigation" column.** Per the brief, only ecosystem-wide or platform-level policy/product changes are recorded. Fixes made by the affected project alone (token rotation, patched release, repo cleanup) are deliberately excluded and shown as `n.a.` when no registry- or platform-level change was tied to the incident by a fetched source.

## Section 4 — Defenses with evidence they reduced repeat incidents

Each item: what it is, who adopted it, what it responded to, and what the evidence actually shows. I separate *adoption* evidence (widely available) from *effectiveness* evidence (much rarer) because the distinction matters for design decisions.

- **Mandatory 2FA (and the move to phishing-resistant factors)**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI required 2FA for "all users" from January 1, 2024, after starting with "the top 1% of projects by downloads" and distributing "4,000 hardware keys" ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-01-01-2fa-enforced/)). npm scoped its requirement to the "top 100 by dependents", then the "top 500", and then to "high-impact packages at a million weekly downloads or 500 dependents" ([Nesbitt](https://nesbitt.io/2026/08/18/two-factor-authentication-across-package-registries.html)). RubyGems requires it for "owners of any gem with more than 180 million total downloads" ([Nesbitt](https://nesbitt.io/2026/08/18/two-factor-authentication-across-package-registries.html)).
  - *Responded to:* the maintainer-account-takeover class generally.
  - *Evidence:* adoption is real and measurable — PyPI reports ">52% active users with non-phishable 2FA" ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-12-31-pypi-2025-in-review/)), up from "~80% of all users that have logged in since January 2024 have 2FA enabled" of any kind ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/pypi-slashes-malware-response-time)). **But TOTP-based 2FA demonstrably did not stop repeats.** Lottie Player was taken over "with 2FA enabled" because a long-lived automation token could publish ([Checkmarx](https://checkmarx.com/blog/with-2fa-enabled-npm-package-lottie-player-taken-over-by-attackers/)), and the Qix compromise came from a phishing email that reset 2FA ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/npm-author-qix-compromised-in-major-supply-chain-attack)). This is precisely why GitHub's post-Shai-Hulud plan **deprecates TOTP in favor of FIDO** ([GitHub](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/our-plan-for-a-more-secure-npm-supply-chain/)) and PyPI pushes "phishing-resistant 2FA" ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-09-23-plenty-of-phish-in-the-sea/)).
  - *Design takeaway for a new registry:* make WebAuthn the only second factor, and do not ship a token type that can publish without it.

- **Trusted publishing / OIDC (short-lived, workflow-scoped credentials instead of long-lived tokens)**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI launched it April 20, 2023 ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2023-04-20-introducing-trusted-publishers/)); RubyGems December 14, 2023 ([RubyGems](https://blog.rubygems.org/2023/12/14/trusted-publishing.html)); npm reached GA July 31, 2025 for GitHub Actions and GitLab CI, with provenance attestations on by default ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-07-31-npm-trusted-publishing-with-oidc-is-generally-available/)); crates.io launched it in 2025, and pub.dev also adopted it ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/crates-launches-trusted-publishing)).
  - *Responded to:* the maintainer-token theft class — Lottie Player, `eslint-config-prettier`, Qix, Nx.
  - *Evidence:* strong adoption numbers — PyPI reports ">50,000 projects using trusted publishing" and ">20% of uploads via trusted publishers" ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-12-31-pypi-2025-in-review/)); crates.io reported "more than 189,000 crates" as of July 2025 ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/crates-launches-trusted-publishing)). npm hard-cut the alternative: it "disabled classic token creation in early November" and "revoked remaining classic tokens" on December 9, and cut session token lifetime from 12h to 2h ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/npm-to-implement-staged-publishing)).
  - **Explicit limits, which are the most important finding in this section.** Trusted publishing did *not* stop the 2026 worms. The May 2026 Mini Shai-Hulud wave's malicious versions "appeared under the legitimate `GitHub Actions` / `<npm-oidc-no-reply@github.com>` publisher identity in npm metadata" ([Mend](https://www.mend.io/blog/mini-shai-hulud-is-back-172-npm-and-pypi-packages-compromised-in-latest-wave/)); ChainDrop "abuses GHA OIDC trusted publishers so packages carry valid provenance" ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/08/04/chaindrop-supply-chain-compromise-anatomy-self-propagating-worm/)); and the `axios` attacker "bypassed OIDC using a long-lived token" that still existed on the account ([Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/supply-chain-compromise-axios-npm-package)). OIDC moves the attack from the maintainer's laptop to the maintainer's CI, and CI is now where the attacks are.

- **Sigstore signing, provenance attestations, and transparency logs**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI shipped PEP 740 attestations to GA on November 14, 2024, implemented jointly by "the Google Open Source Security Team" and "Trail of Bits", with **default enablement** for projects using Trusted Publishing and the canonical GitHub Action ([Sigstore](https://blog.sigstore.dev/pypi-attestations-ga/)). npm attaches provenance attestations automatically for trusted-publishing releases ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-07-31-npm-trusted-publishing-with-oidc-is-generally-available/)).
  - *Responded to:* Ultralytics — and this is the one case with clean causal detection evidence.
  - *Evidence of effect:* the Ultralytics compromise was caught because the malicious artifacts did not match the project's attestations, cross-checked against Sigstore transparency logs ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-12-11-ultralytics-attack-analysis/)). Nx s1ngularity was likewise detected via **missing npm provenance signing** on the malicious versions ([Nx](https://nx.dev/blog/s1ngularity-postmortem)). Adoption is climbing: "over 20,000 individual attestations" and "just over 5% of the top 360 projects" at GA ([Sigstore](https://blog.sigstore.dev/pypi-attestations-ga/)), rising to "86 of the top 360 most-downloaded packages on PyPI ... uploaded with attestations", "up 309% from 21 in November 2024" ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/crates-launches-trusted-publishing)), and PyPI reporting 17% of uploads carrying an attestation ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-12-31-pypi-2025-in-review/)).
  - *Caveat:* provenance proves *where* a build ran, not that the build was clean — see ChainDrop above.

- **Quarantine / hold windows / minimum release age**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI introduced a non-destructive **Quarantine** project state, with "~140 projects quarantined since Aug 2024" and "only 1 exited" quarantine ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-12-30-quarantine/)). Client-side release-age gating shipped across the JS ecosystem: pnpm `minimumReleaseAge` in v10.16 (Sep 2025), Yarn `npmMinimalAgeGate` in 4.10.0 (mid-Sep 2025), Bun in v1.3 (Oct 2025), and npm CLI 11.10.0 `minimumReleaseAge` on February 26, 2026 ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/npm-introduces-minimumreleaseage-and-bulk-oidc-configuration)). npm also moved to **staged publishing** ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/npm-to-implement-staged-publishing)).
  - *Responded to:* the fast-moving worm waves, where the damage window is minutes to hours.
  - *Evidence of effect:* the timing data makes the case arithmetically rather than by vendor claim. `axios`'s first infection was **89 seconds after publish** and detection came at ~6 minutes ([Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/supply-chain-compromise-axios-npm-package)); Postman's hijacked packages were live "for about six hours" ([Postman](https://blog.postman.com/engineering/root-cause-analysis-shai-halud-2-0/)); PostHog's window was 4:11→9:30 UTC ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/handbook/company/post-mortems/2025-11-26-shai-hulud-attack)). PostHog's own recommendation after the incident is "a 3 day" package delay ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/handbook/company/post-mortems/2025-11-26-shai-hulud-attack)). Every one of these would have been absorbed by a multi-day age gate. I found no vendor publishing a measured reduction-in-incidents figure for release-age gating, so treat this as strong mechanism evidence rather than proven outcome evidence.

- **Immutable SHA pinning and allow-listed actions**
  - *Adopted by:* GitHub shipped organization/enterprise policy to block specific actions and to require SHA pinning on August 15, 2025 — "preventing potential malicious updates to existing tags" and giving pipelines "trusted, unalterable assets" ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-08-15-github-actions-policy-now-supports-blocking-and-sha-pinning-actions/)).
  - *Responded to:* tj-actions/changed-files and reviewdog, where mutable tags were repointed to a malicious commit ([StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/harden-runner-detection-tj-actions-changed-files-action-is-compromised), [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/reviewdog-github-actions-are-compromised)).
  - *Evidence of effect:* the mechanism directly neutralizes the tag-repointing primitive, and CISA's guidance in the same period was to pin to known-safe releases ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2025/09/23/widespread-supply-chain-compromise-impacting-npm-ecosystem)). However, SHA pinning did **not** prevent the 2026 Actions-based attacks, which compromised the build environment itself rather than the action reference — angular/dev-infra via cache poisoning ([Adnan Khan](https://adnanthekhan.com/posts/angular-compromise-through-dev-infra/)) and TeamPCP via compromised action repositories ([Datadog](https://securitylabs.datadoghq.com/articles/litellm-compromised-pypi-teampcp-supply-chain-campaign/)). No vendor-published repeat-reduction metric found.

- **Removing or sandboxing install-time scripts**
  - *Adopted by:* GitHub announced on June 11, 2026 that npm would disable install scripts by default, calling lifecycle hooks "the single largest code-execution surface in the npm ecosystem" ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/github-to-disable-npm-install-scripts.html)). **npm 12 disables `preinstall`, `install`, `postinstall` and implicit `node-gyp` by default**, with an opt-in `trusted-script` allowlist in `package.json`; npm v12 also defaults to `--allow-git=none` ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/npm-12-disables-install-scripts-by.html), [Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/npm-introduces-minimumreleaseage-and-bulk-oidc-configuration)).
  - *Responded to:* Shai-Hulud, whose payload "executes only during the pre-install stage" ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/shai-hulud-malware-infects-500-npm-packages-leaks-secrets-on-github/)), and `eslint-config-prettier`'s install-script DLL drop ([StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/supply-chain-security-alert-eslint-config-prettier-package-shows-signs-of-compromise)).
  - *Evidence of effect:* too recent to show a measured reduction, and ChainDrop landed in August 2026 *after* the change ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/08/04/chaindrop-supply-chain-compromise-anatomy-self-propagating-worm/)), executing in CI rather than at install. The crates.io August 2026 compromise is a reminder that removing *install* scripts leaves **build** scripts (`build.rs`, proc macros) as an equivalent surface ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/popular-rust-crates-compromised)), as does Java's ability to execute compiled jars during builds.
  - *Design takeaway:* a new language should treat install-time *and* build-time execution as one surface and default both to off.

- **Namespace / repo-jacking and domain-resurrection protections**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI implemented **Domain Resurrection Prevention** ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-12-31-pypi-2025-in-review/)) and denied the `aiocpa` attacker's attempt to take over an existing project name ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2024-11-25-aiocpa-attack-analysis/)).
  - *Responded to:* the abandoned-asset class documented by Checkmarx — MavenGate, expired maintainer email domains behind `ctx` and `PHPass`, abandoned S3 buckets behind `bignum`, and abandoned gem names like `gemnasium-gitlab-service` ([Checkmarx](https://checkmarx.com/blog/the-hidden-dangers-of-abandoned-digital-assets-in-open-source-ecosystems/)).
  - *Evidence of effect:* **partially ruled out.** PyPI's control addresses the email-domain resurrection vector specifically, and I found no vendor claim quantifying reduced repo-jacking. Maven Central has no equivalent control that I could confirm; Aikido's recommendation after the Jackson typosquat was that Maven Central *should* adopt "prefix-similarity namespace detection", implying it does not ([SC Media](https://www.scworld.com/brief/maven-central-subjected-to-typosquatted-jackson-json-library)). ReversingLabs also documented a VS Code Marketplace loophole permitting reuse of "the name of a legitimate but discontinued VS Code extension" ([ReversingLabs](https://www.reversinglabs.com/blog/sscs-report-2026-takeaways)).

- **Typosquat name blocking at creation time**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI added **typosquatting detection at project creation** ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-12-31-pypi-2025-in-review/)).
  - *Responded to:* the long tail of lookalike packages across every registry.
  - *Evidence of effect:* **not demonstrated.** PyPI publishes the control but no reduction metric, and typosquats continued through 2026 on PyPI (`spellcheckpy`), NuGet, Maven Central, RubyGems and Go ([Aikido](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/malicious-pypi-packages-spellcheckpy-and-spellcheckerpy-deliver-python-rat), [ReversingLabs](https://www.reversinglabs.com/blog/malicious-nuget-package-targets-stripe), [SC Media](https://www.scworld.com/brief/maven-central-subjected-to-typosquatted-jackson-json-library), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/16-typosquatted-rubygems-packages-steal.html)). Socket's assessment of Go is blunt: "The Go registry is insufficient defense against both typosquatting and malicious packages" ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/11-malicious-go-packages-distribute-obfuscated-remote-payloads)). Note also that name-similarity checks miss maintainer-name spoofing (`csnemes` vs `csnemess`) and Cyrillic homoglyphs ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/12/rogue-nuget-package-poses-as-tracerfody.html)).

- **Slopsquatting-specific detection tooling**
  - *Evidence:* **explicitly ruled out for this period.** I found no registry-level control specifically targeting AI-hallucinated package names. The measurement work exists — 19.7% nonexistent recommended packages across 576,000 samples ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/slopsquatting-how-ai-hallucinations-are-fueling-a-new-class-of-supply-chain-attacks)), GPT-5 at "27.8%" version hallucination across 37,000 recommendations ([Sonatype](https://www.sonatype.com/press-releases/sonatype-research-reveals-open-malware-grows-75-percent)) — but the mitigations described are scanner-side, not registry-side. Sonatype frames it under "Synthetic Growth is Not the Same as Innovation" and "non-existent versions" ([Sonatype](https://www.sonatype.com/state-of-the-software-supply-chain/introduction)).

- **Extension marketplace review changes**
  - *Adopted by:* Open VSX removed all GlassWorm extensions **and rotated publisher tokens** as of October 21, 2025 ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/11/glassworm-malware-discovered-in-three.html)).
  - *Evidence of effect:* **largely ruled out.** The token rotation was incident response, not a review-process change, and malicious extensions continued to appear on both marketplaces afterwards — Bitcoin Black and Codo AI were still live on Microsoft's registry in December 2025 ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-vscode-extensions-on-microsofts-registry-drop-infostealers/)), and 73 cloned extensions surfaced in April 2026 with "more than 320 artifacts ... identified since December 21, 2025" ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/04/researchers-uncover-73-fake-vs-code.html)). Sonatype's incident table shows extension-marketplace counts rising rather than falling ([Sonatype](https://www.sonatype.com/state-of-the-software-supply-chain/2026/open-source-malware)).

- **Fast malware triage as a first-class registry function**
  - *Adopted by:* PyPI hired a dedicated Safety & Security Engineer and rebuilt its reporting pipeline. Results: "Approximately 900 projects were removed since mid-March 2024", and "Malware that used to sit there for months is now removed in just hours and oftentimes minutes from publication" ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/pypi-slashes-malware-response-time)). By 2025: ">2000 malware reports", "66% handled <4h", "92% <24h" ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-12-31-pypi-2025-in-review/)).
  - *Evidence of effect:* this is the clearest measured improvement of the whole period, and it is operational capacity rather than a technical control. Complemented by third-party automated detection now catching things in seconds to minutes: Socket flagged `axios` in ~6 minutes ([Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/supply-chain-compromise-axios-npm-package)) and the crates.io compromise at 07:29:50 UTC on publish day ([Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/popular-rust-crates-compromised)); GitLab flagged a malicious PyPI version "within 28 seconds" ([GitLab](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/shai-hulud-copycat-campaign-targets-python-developers/)); Sonatype's Repository Firewall "prevented 136,107 attacks in Q1" 2026 ([Sonatype Q1 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/14/3273135/0/en/sonatype-releases-q1-2026-open-source-malware-index-trust-abuse-most-successful-attack-vector.html)).

- **IoC-based upload blocking and cross-registry collaboration**
  - *Adopted by:* GitHub began "blocking uploads matching known IoCs" after Shai-Hulud ([GitHub](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/our-plan-for-a-more-secure-npm-supply-chain/)); PyPI added registrar takedowns, blocklists, and explicit "cross-registry collaboration" after the phishing wave ([PyPI](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2025-09-23-plenty-of-phish-in-the-sea/)); Maven Central purged mirrored copies of a worm-infected npm-derived artifact within about a day ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/11/shai-hulud-v2-campaign-spreads-from-npm.html)).
  - *Evidence of effect:* npm's own UI began surfacing "a warning message about unauthorized publication of the latest version, indicating that the automated review has caught signs of a compromise" during Shai-Hulud 2.0 ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/shai-hulud-malware-infects-500-npm-packages-leaks-secrets-on-github/)) — evidence the blocking pipeline was live and firing, though not a quantified reduction.

## Known gaps and limitations

- **`n.a.` in the mitigation column is common and meaningful.** For most typosquat and extension incidents, no fetched source describes any ecosystem-level policy change — only takedown of the specific artifact.
- **Protestware inside the window is thin.** The only in-window campaign I could confirm is the 28-package Russian-locale wave tracked by Socket in July 2025. The destructive protestware canon (`node-ipc`, `colors.js`, `faker.js`) predates January 2024.
- **Repo-jacking inside the window is research, not confirmed exploitation.** MavenGate (January 2024) and the Checkmarx abandoned-assets survey document the attack surface; I found no fetched primary source documenting a measured repo-jacking breach with victim counts in this window.
- **Dependency confusion is likely under-represented.** Only the Microsoft May 2026 33-package reconnaissance campaign is confirmed from a primary source.
- **Sonatype's 2026 report introduction page renders its headline figures as `00` placeholders**, so I sourced those numbers from Sonatype's press release and Q1 2026 index instead ([Sonatype](https://www.sonatype.com/state-of-the-software-supply-chain/introduction), [Sonatype press release](https://www.sonatype.com/press-releases/sonatype-research-reveals-open-malware-grows-75-percent), [Sonatype Q1 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/14/3273135/0/en/sonatype-releases-q1-2026-open-source-malware-index-trust-abuse-most-successful-attack-vector.html)).
