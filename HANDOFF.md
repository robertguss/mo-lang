> **What is proven and what is not, plainly.** Proven: the feedback loop (0.38 s to check and test a seven-module service, against 9 and 19 s in Python and Go); zero dependencies; the failure model (a kill under load loses no acknowledged write); capabilities the checker follows through parameters, messages, and branches; a move analysis that writes maps in place (47 s → 0.13 s). Not proven: that Mo's checks make programs more reliable. In six programs no check caught a bug the worker's own tests would not have, and in rounds 6 and 7 Go and Python with checks bolted on were as correct and as durable as Mo. Every program so far was written once and never changed; the laws exist for the second agent, and that has never been measured. The runtime is the bottleneck, not the language: every program finds two to four toolchain bugs and several stdlib gaps; a Mo program runs on one core; replay memory grows faster than the log.
